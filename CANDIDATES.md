# Fix Human Sleep: Funniest Patch Note — Candidates & Tournament

Judge brief (weight order): (1) really a patch note about fixing human
sleep; (2) funny on a single read; (3) genuine changelog voice; (4) single
line, under the 160-char cap, no padding; (5) surprising — a specific,
unexpected fix rather than a flat claim that sleep is fixed.

## Tournament bracket (single elimination, judged against the brief in weight order)

### Round 1
1. **A vs B → A wins (margin 2).** Both on-subject, both funny. A's "replay
   every embarrassing thing they did in 2011" is more surprising and specific
   (criterion 5) than B's battery metaphor, a familiar trope.
2. **C vs D → D wins (margin 2).** C's retired snooze button is a decent fix
   but flat; D's `anxiety.exe` background tab is a funnier, more specific joke
   with a real changelog shape.
3. **E vs F → E wins (margin 2).** E subverts a classic trope with a visual
   punchline (the sheep fall asleep first); F is relatable but its payoff,
   "rest now applies correctly", is weaker.
4. **G vs H → G wins (margin 1).** Close. H's "interest charges remain
   unchanged" is dry and clever, but G's dream auto-save ("waking up no longer
   loses your progress") lands faster and is more surprising.
5. **I vs J → I wins (margin 2).** I has two beats — deprecated doomscrolling
   plus a phone that puts itself to bed — and the "deprecated" wording is
   pitch-perfect changelog voice. J is a one-beat gag.
6. **K vs L → K wins (margin 1).** Close. L's cancelled 2 AM meeting rehearsal
   is relatable, but K's "remembering dreams is still a known issue" is the
   most changelog-authentic kicker in the field.
7. **M vs N → M wins (margin 1).** M's overnight install without a 3 AM restart
   is a cleaner patch-note metaphor; N's "rare drop" is funny but reads less
   like a fix announcement.
8. **O vs P → O wins (margin 2).** O reframes nightmares as a product decision
   (optional DLC) — surprising and funny. P's update-nag is decent but the
   joke is thinner.

### Quarterfinals
9. **A vs D → A wins (margin 2).** Both strong. A's 3 AM cringe replay is the
   more universal laugh — everyone has lived it — while D's `anxiety.exe` is a
   clever but known meme format. A is also more specific ("in 2011").
10. **E vs G → E wins (margin 1).** Close. G's dream auto-save is gamer-fluent,
    but E's sheep falling asleep first lands on a single read for any reader,
    and its twist is more surprising.
11. **I vs K → K wins (margin 1).** Close. I's self-bedding phone is charming,
    but K pairs a bold fix claim (asleep in under 8 seconds) with the dry
    "known issue" kicker — two beats, both in perfect changelog voice.
12. **M vs O → O wins (margin 2).** O's optional-DLC nightmares are funnier and
    more surprising than M's solid but flat overnight-install metaphor.

### Semifinals
13. **A vs E → A wins (margin 2).** E's sheep are cute and the twist is good,
    but the sheep trope is familiar. A's 3 AM embarrassment replay is a
    sharper, more vivid scene and the bigger laugh.
14. **K vs O → O wins (margin 1).** The closest call so far. K's "known issue"
    kicker is the driest wit in the field, but O's whole line is a joke with
    setup and payoff — "nightmares are now optional DLC" reframes the problem
    as a product decision, which is more surprising (criterion 5).

### Final
15. **A vs O → A wins (margin 1).** Both are real patch notes about fixing
    sleep, both funny, both in changelog voice, both under the cap. Decided on
    criteria 1 and 2: A's bug is a *sleep* bug (waking at 3 AM) while O's is a
    *dream* bug, one step removed from fixing sleep; and A's laugh is the more
    universal one — the 3 AM cringe replay needs no gaming vocabulary, and
    "every embarrassing thing they did in 2011" paints a scene the reader
    recognises instantly. O's DLC joke is excellent but narrower. A takes it
    by a hair.

## Champion

**A — 93 characters:**

> Fixed a bug where humans woke up at 3 AM to replay every embarrassing thing they did in 2011.

Why it won: it is the most canonical patch note in the field ("Fixed a bug
where..."), its bug is unambiguously a sleep bug, its joke is the most
universally lived experience in the set, its specificity (3 AM, 2011) makes
it surprising rather than generic, and it never explains itself. It trusts
the reader to have been awake at 3 AM, replaying 2011.
